Which rugs suit pick-up

Pick-up is meant for rugs that need controlled wet cleaning and full drying, which a living room floor cannot provide. These are usually natural-fibre and delicate rugs. A fitted carpet cannot be lifted, so it is cleaned on site only, and large synthetic rugs are usually cleaned at home too, on site.

  • Wool and cotton rugs: with pick-up only.
  • Silk, viscose (also “bamboo silk”), hide, hand-made, Persian and antique rugs: with pick-up only, the price is given from a photo.
  • Jute, sisal and coir rugs: with pick-up, the price is given from a photo.
  • Small synthetic rugs: with pick-up or at home, whichever suits you.

If you do not know what the rug is made of, choose “Not sure, please advise” in the “Where to clean?” field. From photos of the top and the underside, a specialist will recommend where to clean the rug. If there is no care label, the back of the rug says more about the fibre.

Rug washing in a workshop: why natural fibres cannot be cleaned on the floor

Wool is an animal fibre with scales. Hot water together with rubbing or the movement of a washing machine makes wool felt, and the rug may shrink. It usually cannot be reversed. That is why, for a wool rug, the amount of water, the temperature and how long the rug stays damp matter.

Wet viscose becomes much weaker and may change shape. Its pile may turn brownish or yellow from moisture, and flattened pile may stay. Jute and sisal can turn brown in water, and makers advise cleaning them dry. Hide and sheepskin usually suffer from direct water, and heat dries them out and warps them.

For a wool or hand-made rug, colourfastness must be tested on a hidden spot before it is wetted, because the dyes can bleed. Conservators write that high humidity damages fibres and can make dyes bleed. In tufted rugs the yarn is fixed to the backing with latex glue: strong wetting and heat can weaken the glue, and an old backing may crumble.

How long a rug takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the backing, the humidity of the room air and ventilation.

Sources: University of Wisconsin Extension (UW-Extension) on wool felting; Canadian Conservation Institute on rug care and hide; Ohio State University and Cleanfax on viscose; Fibreworks on latex backing. Checked 10.11.2026.

Rug cleaners or cleaning at home

If you are looking for rug cleaners in Riga, pick-up means the same thing: the rug is cleaned in a workshop, and you do not have to take it anywhere. If the rug is large and synthetic or it is a fitted carpet, rug cleaning at home is more suitable. More on the risks of each fibre on the page professional rug cleaning.

We do not do rug repairs, edge binding or fringe restoration, and we do not store rugs. How to prepare a rug for storage is in the article how to wrap and store a rug.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and sand in the pile;
  • everyday dirt and greyness;
  • fresh drink and food stains, if they have not been rubbed.

May stay

  • old stains, paint, hair dye and bleach marks;
  • smell, if urine has reached the backing;
  • flattened viscose pile and wear;
  • marks of earlier shrinking or dye bleeding.

A smell can be reduced, but we cannot promise it will disappear. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect from the particular rug.

Frequently asked questions

Are pick-up and return included in the price?

No. Pick-up and return in Riga costs €10 (both ways together), in the Riga region up to about 15 km from the Riga boundary it is also €10, and further out another €10 is added. This charge is added to the cleaning price and to the €45 minimum order.

How long will the rug be in the workshop?

That depends on the fibre, the pile thickness and the backing, because the rug has to dry completely.

How is the price of a round rug calculated?

By the rectangle around the rug. A round rug 2 m across is 2 × 2 m, or 4 m². Write the diameter in the request.

Can a wool rug be cleaned at home on the floor?

We clean wool and cotton rugs only with pick-up. Hot water and rubbing can make wool felt and shrink, and moisture can make the dyes bleed.

Can a rug be washed in a washing machine?

For a wool rug it is risky, because the movement of the washing machine together with warm water can make wool felt. More in the article can a rug be washed in a washing machine or in the bath.
