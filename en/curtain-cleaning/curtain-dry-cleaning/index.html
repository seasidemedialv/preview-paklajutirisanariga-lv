What the care label says

The label is the main source, because only the maker knows what the fabric is woven from and how it was dyed. Care symbols are set by the GINETEX system and the ISO 3758 standard. A circle with a letter means professional cleaning, bars under the circle mean a gentler process, and a crossed-out symbol means “do not”. If the wash tub is crossed out, the curtain is not meant to be washed in water.

If there is no label, photograph the fabric from both sides, the seam and the lining. In the request, write what you know about the fabric from the shop or the seamstress. This helps to choose the right cleaning.

Sources: GINETEX care symbols brochure, ISO 3758. Checked 07.11.2026.

Velvet curtain cleaning

Velvet has a pile, and that is its most sensitive part. The fabric maker Brentano writes that water and pressure can crush the pile of viscose velvet, so velvet must not be rubbed or steamed. For velvet of other fibres, see the label. A liquid is blotted from the edges towards the centre, and brush the pile only when the fabric is dry, in the direction of the pile.

So do not soak velvet curtains at home or steam them on the rail. If something has spilled on one, blot it with a clean cloth, do not rub, and send a photo.

Lined curtains

A lined curtain has two fabrics sewn together. The face and the lining can be of different fibres, so read both labels if there are any. NC State Extension’s material on textiles notes that soaked fabric often shrinks and changes colour. In a lined curtain this can show as a wavy edge or a pulled-in hem.

If the curtain has already been washed once and the edge has become uneven, write that in the request. Cleaning does not straighten it.

Silk and jacquard curtain cleaning

Silk is a natural fibre. Brentano writes that natural fibres react to air humidity and the length can change by up to 3%. The Canadian Conservation Institute (CCI) notes that high humidity can make dyes bleed, while light dries the fibres and fades the colours. Cleaning does not restore sun fading.

Jacquard is a fabric with a woven pattern, and its fibres can differ. Here the composition on the label decides, not the pattern. If the fabric has threads of several colours, test the colourfastness on a hidden spot before wetting, because the dyes can bleed.

What to do before the request

  • Photograph the curtain from the front and the back, and the label up close.
  • Measure the width and length of each curtain in metres, or write the length of the rail.
  • Write what has spilled on the fabric and whether you have already tried to clean the stain.
  • Do not try to wash velvet, silk or a lined curtain in a washing machine if the label does not allow it.

Test any product on a hidden spot first, because the dye can bleed. If it is hard to take the curtains down yourself, see curtain take-down, cleaning and rehanging.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and greyness in velvet and jacquard;
  • fresh stains that have not been rubbed;
  • kitchen and smoke smell may fade.

May stay

  • crushed or rubbed velvet pile;
  • sun fading on silk and coloured fabrics;
  • water marks and a wavy lining after earlier washing;
  • dye that has already bled onto a lighter background.

Frequently asked questions

Can a curtain marked for professional cleaning be washed in a washing machine?

The maker does not intend it to be machine-washed. When soaked, the fabric can shrink, change colour or keep water marks, and velvet pile can be crushed. If the curtain is expensive or made to order, the risk is greater than the gain.

What if the curtain has no label?

Send photos of both sides, the lining and the seam, and write what you know about the fabric. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

Will a velvet curtain look like new after cleaning?

We do not promise that. Dust and fresh stains can usually be reduced, but crushed pile and sun fading may stay.

Can only the lining or only the face be cleaned?

A lined curtain is sewn together, so it is usually cleaned as one curtain. The price is calculated by the fabric area as for a heavy curtain, from €3.70/m².
