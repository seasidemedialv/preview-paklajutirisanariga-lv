Why a long pile is a job of its own

In a long, thick pile, sand and crumbs get deep between the fibres, and a thick pile together with the backing usually holds water longer. That is why moisture is the main question for a shaggy rug: how much of it gets into the rug and whether it can be dried completely. How long a rug takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the backing, the room temperature, the humidity and the ventilation.

Shaggy is a type of pile, not a fibre. A shaggy rug can be synthetic, wool or viscose, and each fibre has its own risks. Wool can felt from hot water together with rubbing, while wet viscose becomes much weaker and can turn brown. So the label or a photo of the underside matters more than the word “shaggy”.

How to look after a shaggy rug day to day

  • Vacuum regularly: dirt collects deeper in a long pile than in a short one.
  • Turn the rug from time to time. Conservators advise turning rugs so that they wear evenly.
  • Do not drag the rug by one edge and do not drag furniture across it.
  • Blot spilled liquid with a clean cloth and do not rub: rubbing pushes the stain deeper.
  • Do not use shop-bought carpet shampoos on valuable rugs. The Canadian Conservation Institute writes that afterwards dirt sticks to the fibres even more.

Washing a shaggy rug at home: what can go wrong

A thick rug in the bath or a washing machine is soaked right through, including the backing, and then it stays damp for a long time. A porous material in which mould has started to grow often cannot be cleaned completely, and the US Environmental Protection Agency writes that such materials sometimes have to be thrown away. For a tufted rug, heavy wetting and heat can weaken the backing glue. Before trying anything, read can a rug go in the washing machine.

What to write in the request

  • The size: length × width in metres, without the fringe. For a round rug, write the diameter.
  • The pile length: with a ruler, measure one tuft from the backing to the tip.
  • The fibre, if you know it, and a photo of the label.
  • Photos of the top and the underside, stains, smell, pets.
  • Which floor the room is on, whether there is a lift and where a car can stop.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust, sand and crumbs in the pile;
  • everyday dirt and greyness;
  • fresh stains, if they have not been rubbed.

May stay

  • a flattened or tangled pile where furniture has stood or where people walk most;
  • paint, hair dye and bleach marks;
  • smell, if urine has reached the backing;
  • an old stain that has already soaked into the backing.

A flattened viscose pile may stay. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Can a shaggy rug be cleaned at home?

A large synthetic shaggy rug can be, but this depends on the fibre, the backing and the size. Wool and viscose long-pile rugs are cleaned in a workshop with pick-up.

Why does a long-pile rug cost more than an ordinary synthetic one?

The pile length and thickness affect the price. A synthetic rug with a short pile is from €6/m² at home, and a long-pile or thick rug from €10/m².

Will a flattened pile stand up again after cleaning?

That cannot be promised. Dirt can usually be reduced, but a pile that has been pressed for years under furniture or worn down may stay the same.

How do I measure the pile length?

Push the pile to one side and measure one tuft from the backing to the tip with a ruler. Write the number in centimetres in the request, together with the size of the rug.
