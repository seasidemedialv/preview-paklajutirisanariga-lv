Cotton is a natural fibre, and textiles that get wet often shrink or bleed dye. The same applies to flatweave rugs (kilims) made of cotton or wool. A synthetic flatweave rug is a different case, and it can also be cleaned at home.

What a flatweave rug is and what it can be made of

A flatweave rug has no pile: it is a woven flat rug, often called a kilim. Cotton rugs are often woven or braided. But a flatweave rug can also be made of wool, jute or synthetics, and the fibre decides where and how it is cleaned.

Look at the label. If there is none, photograph the rug from the top and the underside, and a specialist tells you from the photos what it might be. Jute and sisal have their own page: jute and sisal rug cleaning.

Why a cotton rug is cleaned in a workshop

The North Carolina State University extension service (NC State Extension) writes that textiles that get wet often change colour, bleed dye, shrink and get lasting water marks. For a multicoloured cotton rug this means that a dark colour can run into the light stripes.

Natural fibres need controlled wet cleaning and full drying, which a living room floor does not give. That is why cotton and wool rugs are cleaned in a workshop with pick-up. Colourfastness should be tested on a hidden spot before wetting, because the dyes can bleed.

A wool kilim and a synthetic flatweave rug

A wool kilim has the risks of wool: hot water together with rubbing or the movement of a washing machine felts the wool, and the rug can shrink. Usually it cannot be reversed. The price is the same as for cotton, from €10/m², and there are more details on the page wool rug cleaning.

A synthetic flatweave rug can also be cleaned at your home, from €6/m², or in a workshop with pick-up, from €10/m². For this case: synthetic rug cleaning.

Washing a cotton rug in a washing machine

A small cotton rug can go in a washing machine only if the care label allows it. A cross over the symbol means “do not”. If there is no label, or the rug is large and multicoloured, the risk of shrinking and dye bleeding is high. Before the rug is picked up

  • Measure the rug: length × width in metres, without the fringe.
  • Add photos of the top, the underside and the label, if there is one.
  • Write whether the rug has already been washed and whether it changed afterwards.
  • Mention stains, smell and pets.
  • Photograph damage and loose fringe before cleaning. We do not do repairs or fringe restoration.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and everyday dirt;
  • greyness in walking areas;
  • fresh drink and food stains, if they have not been rubbed.

May stay

  • dye that bled in an earlier wash;
  • shrinking after washing in a washing machine;
  • paint, hair dye and bleach marks;
  • wear and loose threads.

A fresh stain usually comes out better than an old one. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Why is a cotton rug not cleaned at home on the floor?

Cotton can shrink and bleed dye when wet, and it needs controlled wet cleaning and full drying. A living room floor does not give that, so cotton rugs are cleaned in a workshop with pick-up.

Can a cotton rug that has already shrunk be brought back to its old size?

That cannot be promised. Write in the request how the rug was washed and what changed, and add photos. A specialist tells you what to expect.

How do I tell whether a kilim is wool or cotton?

First look at the label. If there is none, send photos of both sides. For both fibres, cleaning in a workshop is from €10/m², but the risks differ.

How much does it cost to clean a small cotton rug?

Cleaning is from €10/m², but the minimum order is €45: if the total is lower, you pay €45. Pick-up and return in Riga costs €10 and is added.
