How to tell that a rug is synthetic

The label or the purchase details tell you most reliably. Synthetic fibres are polypropylene, polyester, nylon (polyamide) and acrylic. Many rugs are sold as a “polyester rug” or a “nylon rug”, and it is the same job: synthetics with a short pile.

Many rugs have no label, or it has been cut off. Then photograph the rug from the top and the underside: the backing shows whether the rug is woven, tufted with a glued backing or knotted. If the label mentions wool, viscose or “bamboo silk”, this page does not apply: see wool rug cleaning or viscose rug cleaning.

What is different about a synthetic rug

For synthetics, the main question is usually not the fibre itself but the backing and the moisture. In a tufted rug, the yarn is pushed through the backing fabric and fixed with latex glue. Heavy wetting and heat can weaken this glue, and in an old rug the backing can crumble or give off a smell. There is a separate page about this: tufted rug cleaning.

Makers of fitted carpet, for example Shaw, warn that over-wetting without extracting the moisture can cause wrinkles and, in extreme cases, delamination of the backing. Some synthetic fibres melt in heat, so an iron or a hot object on a stain is a risk. How long a rug takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the humidity of the room air and the ventilation.

What to do before cleaning at home

  • Take chairs, small tables and plant pots off the rug. Write about heavy furniture on the rug in the request.
  • Measure the rug: length × width in metres, without the fringe.
  • Add photos of the top and the underside, and of the label, if there is one.
  • Write about stains, smell and pets.
  • In the request, write which floor the room is on, whether there is a lift and where a car can stop.

If something has just been spilled on a synthetic rug

Blot the liquid with a clean, absorbent cloth and do not rub: rubbing pushes the stain deeper. Then put clean absorbent material on the spot and press it with something heavy until it dries. For blood use only cold water, because heat sets it. Chlorine bleach and strong cleaners change the colour. Test any product on a hidden spot first. More: carpet stain removal.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and sand in the pile;
  • fresh drink and food stains, if they have not been rubbed;
  • general greyness in walking areas, if the fibre is not worn.

May stay

  • paint, hair dye and bleach marks;
  • a worn or flattened pile in the walking path;
  • fibre melted by a hot object;
  • smell, if urine has reached the backing.

A fresh stain usually comes out better than an old one. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Can a synthetic rug be cleaned at home on the floor?

Yes, a synthetic rug with a short pile can be cleaned at your home, from €6/m². Before that, take chairs, small tables and plant pots off it, and write about heavy furniture in the request.

How do I know whether a rug is polyester or nylon?

Look at the label. If there is none, send photos of the top and the underside, and a specialist tells you what it might be and where to clean it. For us, polyester, nylon, polypropylene and acrylic are one price line.

How much does it cost to clean a small synthetic rug?

At home it is from €6/m², but the minimum order is €45: if the total is lower, you pay €45. With pick-up, cleaning is from €10/m², and pick-up and return in Riga costs €10.

Does a synthetic rug need separate odour treatment?

Only if there is urine or smell. It is extra, from €3/m² of the treated area. The smell can be reduced, but nobody can promise it will go away, especially if urine has reached the backing.
