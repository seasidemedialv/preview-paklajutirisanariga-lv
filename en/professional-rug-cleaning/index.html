What cleaning by material means

Professional rug cleaning is not one method that can be used on any rug. What matters is the fibre of the rug and its dyes, not the name of the product. Wool can felt and shrink from hot water and rubbing, viscose becomes much weaker when wet, and jute can turn brown in water. So before cleaning you need to know the fibre, the backing and the pile length: they decide how much moisture the rug tolerates.

The second thing is the backing. In a tufted rug the yarn is pulled into a fabric backing and fixed with latex glue, while a hand-knotted rug has no glued layer, and the fringe is the ends of the warp threads. That is why a photo of the underside can tell you more than a photo of the top.

At home or in a workshop: what decides

At home we clean fitted carpets, because they cannot be lifted, and large synthetic rugs. With pick-up we clean wool, cotton, silk, viscose, hide, hand-made, Persian and antique rugs, and also jute and sisal rugs. The reason is simple: delicate fibres need controlled wet cleaning and full drying, which a living room floor does not give.

With pick-up, the rug is cleaned in a workshop and brought back. If you are looking for a rug cleaning workshop, this is that option, except that you do not have to bring the rug yourself. How to tell what your rug is made of

If the rug has a care label, start with it. Care symbols are set by the GINETEX system and the standard ISO 3758: a cross over a symbol means “do not”, letters in a circle denote the professional cleaning types, and bars under a symbol mean gentler treatment.

Many rugs have no label. Then photograph the rug from the top and from the underside, and a corner in close-up. The underside shows whether the backing is glued fabric, and the fringe shows whether it is sewn on or is the ends of the warp threads. If you know where and how the rug was bought, write that too.

Deep cleaning and steam cleaning of rugs

The fibre decides how deep, and with how much moisture, the cleaning may go. Steam and hot water are not suitable for all rugs.

  • Wool: hot water together with rubbing can make it felt, and the rug may shrink.
  • Viscose: the pile must not be rubbed or steamed, because water and pressure can crush it for good.
  • Jute and sisal: makers advise cleaning them dry, not with steam.
  • Tufted rug: a lot of moisture and heat can weaken the glue in the backing.

What not to do before cleaning

The Canadian Conservation Institute advises against using shop-bought carpet shampoos on important rugs, because afterwards dirt sticks to the fibres more easily. Washing a rug in the bath or a washing machine is not suitable for wool, silk or viscose. Also do not dry a rug on a radiator. Heat strips wool of its natural oils and weakens the threads.

Only blot a fresh stain with a clean cloth, without rubbing. Before using any product on a wool or hand-made rug, test colourfastness on a hidden spot, because the dyes can bleed.

  • Photos from the top and from the underside.

Before cleaning, photograph any damage. We do not do repairs or fringe restoration.

What to expect from cleaning

Usually comes out

  • dust and everyday dirt in the pile;
  • fresh stains that have not been rubbed or heated;
  • greyness in the places where people walk, if the fibre is not worn.

May stay

  • paint, hair dye and bleach marks;
  • flattened viscose pile;
  • brown water marks on jute;
  • wear and sun fading;
  • smell, if urine has reached the backing.

A fresh stain usually comes out better than an old one. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect from your rug.

Frequently asked questions

Does “dry cleaning” a rug mean no water is used?

Not always. How much moisture a rug tolerates depends on the fibre. For jute and sisal, makers advise dry cleaning.

Why is the price of a delicate rug given from a photo?

For silk, hand-made and antique rugs the price is given from a photo, because the size, the dyes, the backing and the stains differ greatly between rugs. The starting price is from €15/m², and pick-up and return in Riga costs €10.

Is steam cleaning suitable for all rugs?

No. Viscose pile must not be steamed, makers do not advise steam for jute and sisal, and wool can felt from heat and rubbing. Write the fibre or add a photo, and a specialist will tell you what suits.

Can you repair fringe or a worn edge?

No, we do not do repairs or fringe restoration. Before cleaning, photograph loose fringe and damaged places and add the photos to the request.
