Natural silk or artificial silk

Before the price, you need to know whether the rug really is made of silk. Rugs sold as “bamboo silk” or “artificial silk” are usually made of viscose. That is a different fibre with different risks: when wet it becomes much weaker, can turn brown, and the pile can flatten. For such a rug, see viscose rug cleaning.

What to look at: the care label, if there is one, the purchase documents or the shop description, and the underside of the rug. Many rugs have no label, so add photos of the top and the underside and a close-up of a corner. If the rug is hand-knotted, there is no glued fabric underneath, and the fringe is formed by the ends of the warp threads.

What is risky for a silk rug

The Canadian Conservation Institute writes about rugs that high humidity can damage the fibres and make the colours bleed, while light dries the fibres and fades the colours. For a silk rug this means two things: a liquid on it can leave a mark, and dyes can bleed from water. So a silk rug is not soaked or cleaned on the floor.

Hand-made and antique rugs can have natural dyes that bleed from moisture. Textile specialists at the University of Georgia advise testing any product on a hidden spot first to see whether the colour bleeds.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute, “Basic care: carpets and rugs”; University of Georgia Extension. Checked 10.11.2026.

If something was spilled on a silk rug

  1. Blot Press lightly with a clean, absorbent cloth and do not rub. Rubbing pushes the stain deeper.
  2. Do not use heat Do not dry it with a hair dryer and do not put it on a radiator.
  3. Do not pour on water or products On silk even clean water can leave a mark, and an untested product can make the dye bleed.
  4. Photograph it Add a photo of the stain and the underside to the request and write what was spilled and when.

What to expect from silk rug cleaning

Usually comes out

  • dust and everyday dirt in the pile;
  • a fresh stain that has not been rubbed or treated with products.

May stay

  • water marks that have already dried;
  • dye that has already bled;
  • fading from the sun, and wear;
  • damaged fringe and edges.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know whether my rug is silk or viscose?

Look at the label and the purchase documents. If the rug was sold as “bamboo silk”, it is usually viscose. If there is neither a label nor documents, add photos of both sides, and a specialist advises.

Can a silk rug be cleaned at my home?

No. A silk rug is cleaned in a workshop and brought back, because a delicate fibre needs controlled wet cleaning and full drying.

Why is there no exact price for a silk rug straight away?

The starting price is from €15/m², but silk rugs differ a lot in size, dyes and condition. So the final price is given from a photo.

Will the silk shine as before after cleaning?

We do not promise that. Cleaning can remove dirt, but it does not restore wear, sun fading or dye that has already bled.
