Why plant fibres do not tolerate water

Jute, sisal and seagrass are plant fibres. Water can leave brown patches on them, and this is often explained by lignin, which is in the fibre. Makers sell such rugs marked “dry cleaning only” and do not recommend steam. One maker writes it outright: never use water or any other liquid.

This means that ordinary wet cleaning, which suits a synthetic rug, can give jute exactly the problem you wanted to fix. If the rug already has water marks, it is important not to try to wash them out again.

Care instructions from makers of plant-fibre rugs (for example The Perfect Rug): “dry cleaning only”, no water and no steam. Checked 10.11.2026.

What to do if something is spilled on jute or sisal

  1. Blot at once With a clean, dry, absorbent cloth, blot up as much liquid as you can. Do not rub: rubbing pushes the stain deeper.
  2. Press Put clean absorbent material on the spot and press it with something heavy until it dries.
  3. Do not pour water Do not pour water on it or use steam to “dilute” the stain. Chlorine bleach and strong cleaners change the colour.
  4. Send a photo If a patch remains, photograph it and send it with the form instead of trying again.

Coir and bamboo rugs

Coir is also a plant fibre. A specialist gives the price and the risk for a coir rug from a photo, because they depend on the fibre and the backing.

“Bamboo rug” can mean two different things. If the label says “bamboo silk”, it is usually viscose, and it has another page: viscose rug cleaning. If the rug is made of bamboo slats, send photos of both sides.

What to send in the request

  • The size: length × width in metres. For a round rug, write the diameter.
  • Photos of the top and the underside, and the label if there is one.
  • Close-up photos of stains and water marks.
  • Whether the rug has already been dampened or washed, and with what.
  • Where it is: in the entrance hall, the living room or on a terrace. Which floor the room is on, whether there is a lift and where a car can stop.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and sand between the fibres;
  • dry dirt on the surface;
  • fresh stains, if they were blotted at once and not rubbed.

May stay

  • brown patches and water marks;
  • greasy and old stains that have soaked into the fibre;
  • wear in walking areas and crumbled fibres;
  • colour change from the sun.

A fresh stain usually comes out better than an old one. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Can a jute rug be washed?

Makers advise against it: jute and sisal can turn brown from water, and makers advise cleaning them dry. Washing in the bath or a washing machine can leave brown patches.

Can a jute rug be cleaned at home on the floor?

Jute and sisal rugs are usually cleaned in a workshop with pick-up and return. If the rug is large, write the size and add photos, and a specialist tells you where to clean it and what it costs.

Why is there no m² price for a jute rug?

Plant-fibre rugs differ a lot in fibre, backing and stains, so the price is given from a photo.

Can the brown water patches on sisal be cleaned?

That cannot be promised. Water marks on plant fibres may stay. Send a close-up photo, and a specialist tells you what to expect.

Can a sisal rug be steam cleaned?

Makers do not recommend steam for plant-fibre rugs. More on the methods: dry, wet or steam carpet cleaning.
