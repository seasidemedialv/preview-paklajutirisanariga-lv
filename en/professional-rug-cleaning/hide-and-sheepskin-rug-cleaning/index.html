This applies to cowhide rugs with hair, sheepskins and other rugs on a leather backing. Most leathers are damaged by direct contact with water, and heat dries and deforms the leather.

What is different about leather compared with textiles

The backing of a textile rug is fabric. The backing of a hide rug is the hide itself, and it is the most sensitive part. Care advice for leather is consistent: the Canadian Conservation Institute and the US National Park Service museum handbook say the same. Both sources are about museum objects, but the principle also applies to a hide rug at home.

In practice this means that the leather side must not be soaked, and damp leather must not be dried on a radiator or with a hairdryer.

Canadian Conservation Institute, “Caring for leather, skin and fur”, and US National Park Service, “Museum Handbook”, Appendix S. Checked 07.11.2026.

Cowhide rug and sheepskin

A cowhide rug has short hair, and the main risk is the leather side. A sheepskin has long wool on the leather, so it has two risks at once: the leather does not tolerate water and heat, while the wool felts in hot water combined with rubbing or the movement of a washing machine. Felting usually cannot be undone.

Faux sheepskin is not leather: it is a synthetic rug with a long pile, and shaggy rug cleaning applies to it. If you do not know which you have, turn the rug over: a photo is enough to tell a leather side from a fabric backing.

What not to do with a hide rug at home

  • Do not wash it in the bath or a washing machine unless the maker’s label clearly allows it.
  • Do not dry it on a radiator, by a stove or with a hairdryer: heat dries and deforms the leather.
  • Do not rub the sheepskin wool with a damp cloth.
  • Do not use shop-bought carpet shampoos: the Canadian Conservation Institute advises against them for important rugs.

If something has been spilled, blot the liquid with a clean, dry cloth, do not rub, and let the spot dry at room temperature. If the leather stays hard or turns darker, send a photo.

What to send in the request

  • Photos of the hair side and the leather side.
  • The size: the greatest length and width in metres, because the shape of a hide is irregular.
  • Close-up photos of stains, hard or cracked areas and bald patches.
  • Whether the rug has already been washed or has been wet.
  • Photograph any damage before cleaning.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and dirt in the hair;
  • greyness in sheepskin wool;
  • recent dry dirt.

May stay

  • hard, cracked or shrunken areas of leather after earlier washing;
  • felted sheepskin wool;
  • places where the hair has already fallen out;
  • an old stain on the leather side.

Frequently asked questions

Can a sheepskin go in the washing machine?

Only if the maker’s label clearly allows it. Leather is damaged by direct water, and wool felts in hot water combined with the movement of the machine. If there is no label, do not wash it.

Why is the price for a hide rug given from a photo?

Hide rugs differ in the leather, the hair, the size and the condition. Cleaning is from €15/m², and a specialist gives the final price from photos of both sides.

Can a hide rug be cleaned at home on the floor?

No, hide and sheepskin rugs are cleaned only with pick-up. With pick-up the rug is cleaned in a workshop and brought back.
