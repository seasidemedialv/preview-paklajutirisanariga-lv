Dust mite allergens build up in the settled dust in carpets, bedding and soft furnishings, and mites come back while the air humidity is high. We clean carpets and rugs in Riga and the Riga region at home or with pick-up, a synthetic rug at home from €6/m², a fitted carpet from €4/m².

What carpet cleaning can and cannot give an allergy sufferer

A carpet collects dust, and dust mite allergens are found right in the settled dust. Cleaning reduces this dust in the carpet. However, an overview of combined measures concluded that even several measures together (covers, humidity control, vacuuming) often did not improve allergy symptoms.

So carpet cleaning is best seen as one of the steps of cleaning a home, not as a treatment. For allergy treatment, and for what helps in your case, talk to a doctor.

Dust mites in a carpet and air humidity

The Health Inspectorate (Veselības inspekcija) writes that dust mites multiply in mattresses, bedding and soft furnishings when the relative air humidity is 50–70%. A comfortable humidity in a room is 40–60%. Doctors' practice guidelines state that humidity above 50% helps mites and that acaricides (products that kill mites) are not recommended.

This means that a clean carpet in a damp room will in time become home to mites again. Air the rooms every day and, if you can, monitor the humidity with a hygrometer.

Sources: Health Inspectorate, Health risks, preventive and corrective measures; practice guidelines on environmental control (PMC5156485); an overview of combined measures (PMC12379700). Checked 07.11.2026.

How to deal with dust mites in a carpet

  • Keep the air humidity at 40–60% and air the rooms every day.
  • Vacuum the carpet regularly. Allergy UK advises a vacuum cleaner with a high-efficiency filter.
  • Plan on several measures together: Allergy UK writes that one measure alone is usually not enough.
  • In the bedroom, where the allergic person spends the most time, plan the carpet cleaning together with the bedding and the mattress.

Source: Allergy UK, House Dust Mite Allergy factsheet. Checked 07.11.2026.

Which carpet to clean at home and which with pick-up

The type of cleaning is decided by the rug, not by the allergy. We clean a fitted carpet and large synthetic rugs at your home. Wool, cotton, silk, viscose and hand-made rugs are cleaned with pick-up in a workshop and brought back. A smaller synthetic rug can also be cleaned with pick-up if that is easier for you. In the form, under “Where to clean?”, choose what suits you, or “Not sure, please advise”.

High humidity helps both mites and mould, so after cleaning at home air the room. How long a rug takes to dry depends on the fibre, the thickness of the pile, the humidity of the room air and the ventilation.

What to write in the request

The size of the rug in m², the fibre if you know it, and photos of the top and the underside. Say whether there are pets at home, and if there is hair in the carpet, see also cleaning cat and dog hair from a rug. If the carpet smells of damp, see the page carpet smell cleaning.

Frequently asked questions

Will the carpet be free of dust mites after cleaning?

We cannot promise that. Cleaning reduces the dust and allergens in the carpet, but mites come back while the air humidity is high.

Is it better for an allergy sufferer not to have a carpet at all?

That is a question for a doctor. Doctors' guidelines say that mite allergens are also in bedding and soft furnishings, so a carpet is only one of the places.

Do you also clean mattresses for allergy sufferers?

We clean rugs, fitted carpets and curtains. For cleaning sofas, mattresses and chairs, see mebelutirisanariga.lv.

How often should an allergy sufferer clean a carpet?

Regular vacuuming and air humidity of 40–60% are the everyday part, but plan a full cleaning by how dirty the carpet is and on a doctor’s advice. See also the page how often to clean a carpet.
