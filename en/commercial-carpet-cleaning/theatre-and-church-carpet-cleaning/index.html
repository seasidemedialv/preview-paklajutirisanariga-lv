Aisle runners and the auditorium

In an auditorium the carpet wears unevenly. The aisles and the steps between the rows are walked on, while under the seats the carpet is almost untouched. Between fixed rows of seats access is narrow, and that affects the work and the price, so add photos of the rows and the aisles to the request.

An aisle runner can be fitted or a loose runner fixed at the edges. A loose runner can be cleaned on site or in a workshop; that depends on the fibre, the length and how the runner is fixed. Write the length and width of the runner in metres and, if you know, how it is held to the floor.

Working between events

In a hall with a show or a screening every evening, the cleaning has to fit between events and rehearsals. In the request, say when the rooms are free, for example days without shows or a break in the season.

How long a carpet takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the room humidity and the ventilation. Open the hall to the audience only when the carpet is dry, and allow more time for thick pile than for thin.

Church carpet cleaning

In a church the carpets are often in the central aisle and by the altar, and some of them may be old hand-knotted or Persian rugs. These are not cleaned on site. In hand-knotted, antique and Persian rugs the dyes can bleed, so colourfastness must be tested on a hidden spot before the rug is wetted. For silk, hand-made and antique rugs, the price is set from a photo.

The Canadian Conservation Institute writes that light dries fibres and fades colours, and high humidity damages fibres and can make dyes bleed. Conservators advise turning rugs from time to time so that they wear evenly. We do not do repairs or fringe restoration.

A historic building and access

In an old building access can be harder: narrow stairs, balconies, no lift. In the request, write which floor the room is on, whether there is a lift and where a car can stop. If the building has special rules for work in the interior, attach them to the request.

Before cleaning, photograph any damage, wear and old stains, so that it is clear afterwards what was there already.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and street dirt in the runners;
  • greyness in the aisles and the foyer;
  • fresh drink stains, if they have not been rubbed.

May stay

  • worn pile in the aisles and on the steps;
  • colour faded by light;
  • candle wax marks that are pressed in deep;
  • dye that has already bled.

Frequently asked questions

Can an old church rug be cleaned on site?

No. Hand-knotted, antique and Persian rugs are cleaned in a workshop with pick-up, because the dyes can bleed. The price is set from a photo.

Will a worn aisle runner look like new after cleaning?

No. Cleaning removes dirt, but it does not restore worn pile or colour faded by light.

Do you repair worn rug edges and fringes?

No, we do not do repairs or fringe restoration. We clean carpets and curtains.

Can stage curtains be cleaned too?

Yes, stage curtains are priced case by case. Fire-retardant treatment is not part of this service. If both carpets and curtains need cleaning, put them in one request. For curtains: curtain cleaning for hotels, restaurants and offices.
