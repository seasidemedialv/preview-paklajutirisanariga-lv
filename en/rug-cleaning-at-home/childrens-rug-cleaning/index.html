What is different about a child’s room rug

A children’s rug is played on, eaten on and sometimes slept on. Crumbs, sand from toys and sticky juice stains collect in it, and, while a child is learning to use the potty, urine too. A play rug can be synthetic, cotton or wool, and that decides where and how it may be cleaned.

Some rugs have a care label. A crossed-out symbol means “not allowed”, and letters in a circle mark the types of professional cleaning. If there is no label, photograph the rug from the underside: it helps to identify the fibre.

First aid for stains in a child’s room

  1. Blot, do not rub Blot up as much liquid as you can with a clean, absorbent cloth. Rubbing pushes the stain deeper.
  2. Cold water For blood and other protein stains use cold water, never hot: heat sets the stain.
  3. Chewing gum It is usually hardened with ice in a plastic bag and then scraped off.
  4. Test the product Before using any product, test it on a hidden spot. Chlorine bleach and strong products change the colour of the rug.

More on different stains in the article how to remove a stain from a carpet.

Urine on a children’s rug

Blot fresh urine up at once and put a clean, dry cloth with a weight on the spot until it no longer soaks up moisture. If the urine has reached the backing, the smell can return even after cleaning. Urine or odour treatment costs extra, from €3/m². More on the page carpet urine cleaning.

After cleaning: when the rug is ready for play again

Let the rug dry completely before children play on it again. How long a rug takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the backing, the room temperature, the humidity and the ventilation. A fan and a dehumidifier speed up drying; in humid air water evaporates more slowly.

If a child has a dust mite allergy, note that cleaning reduces the dust and allergens in a rug, but it is not a treatment, and mites return while the air humidity is high. More on the page carpet cleaning for allergy sufferers.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • crumbs, sand and dust in the pile;
  • fresh juice and food stains, if they have not been rubbed;
  • greyness where children play the most.

May stay

  • felt-tip, paint and hair dye stains;
  • urine smell, if the urine has reached the backing;
  • bleach patches after attempts to clean it yourself;
  • worn or flattened pile.

A fresh stain usually responds better than an old one, so it is better to send the request soon.

Frequently asked questions

When can a child play on the rug again?

When the rug is completely dry. How long it takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the room temperature, the humidity and the ventilation.

Can a children’s rug go in the washing machine?

Only if the care label allows it. For a wool rug, the movement of the washing machine together with warm water can felt the fibre and the rug can shrink. More in the article can a rug go in the washing machine or the bath.

Does cleaning a rug help a child with an allergy?

Cleaning reduces the dust and allergens in a rug, but it is not a treatment for an allergy. Talk to a doctor about treatment.

Can I request just one small rug?

Yes. The minimum order is €45: if the total is lower, you pay €45. If you have several rugs, list them in one request.
