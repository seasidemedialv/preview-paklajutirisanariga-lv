Which rugs suit cleaning on site

On site we clean rugs whose fibre tolerates moisture and which are hard or impossible to move. A fitted carpet cannot be lifted, so it is cleaned only where it is laid. A large rug is awkward to roll up and carry down a stairwell, so a large synthetic rug is also usually cleaned on the floor.

  • Synthetic rugs with a short pile: polypropylene, polyester, nylon, acrylic.
  • Fitted carpet (carpeting) and soft floor covering in a flat, in a house and on stairs.
  • Long-pile (shaggy) or thick synthetic rugs: at home or with pick-up, depending on the rug.
  • Small synthetic rugs: at home or with pick-up, whichever suits you.

Which rugs are not cleaned at home

Wool, cotton, silk, viscose, hide, hand-made, Persian and antique rugs, and also jute and sisal, are cleaned with pick-up only. Wool, cotton, silk, viscose and hide need controlled wet cleaning and full drying, which a living room floor cannot provide; jute and sisal can turn brown in water, and makers advise cleaning them dry.

The reasons are in the fibre. Hot water and rubbing can make wool felt and shrink. Wet viscose becomes much weaker, and its pile may turn brown. Jute and sisal can turn brown in water. For these rugs see rug cleaning with pick-up and return, and for an explanation of the fibres see the page professional rug cleaning.

Rug cleaning in a flat

In a flat, access matters. In the request, write which floor the flat is on, whether there is a lift and where a car can stop. If the rug is in a room where people live and walk, think about how to use the room while the rug dries.

Before cleaning at home, remove chairs, small tables and plant pots from the rug. Write about heavy furniture on the rug in the request. When you put furniture back, lift it rather than drag it across the rug: conservators advise not to drag furniture across a rug.

How long a rug takes to dry depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the backing, the room temperature, the air humidity and ventilation. In humid air water evaporates more slowly, while a fan and a dehumidifier speed up drying. More in how long a rug takes to dry after cleaning.

What moisture means for the rug and the floor

A thick pile and backing hold water longer than a thin rug. If urine has gone right through to the backing, the underlay or the floor, cleaning the rug may not end the smell, and the underlay may have to be replaced. The Fibreworks page on latex backing says that latex backing can stick to some new floor finishes, leave colour marks or crumble. If there is a new parquet or lacquer finish under the rug, write that in the request.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust, sand and everyday dirt;
  • greyness along the most-walked routes;
  • fresh stains, if they have not been rubbed.

May stay

  • old stains, paint, hair dye and bleach marks;
  • urine smell, if the urine has reached the backing or the underlay;
  • wear and flattened pile.

Frequently asked questions

How can I clean a rug at home?

Vacuum regularly. Blot a fresh stain with a clean cloth and do not rub. For a protein stain, such as blood, use cold water, not hot. Before using any product, test it on a hidden spot. Do not soak a wool rug or wash it in hot water. More in how to remove a stain from a rug.

Does the rug have to be cleared of furniture at home?

Remove chairs, small tables and plant pots. For heavy furniture on the rug, such as a wardrobe or a sofa, write in the request and add a photo.

Can a wool rug also be cleaned at home?

No. Wool and cotton rugs are cleaned with pick-up only, because hot water and rubbing can make wool felt and shrink.

Will you also clean a sofa or a mattress?

We do not clean sofas and mattresses. For sofa, mattress and chair cleaning see mebelutirisanariga.lv.
