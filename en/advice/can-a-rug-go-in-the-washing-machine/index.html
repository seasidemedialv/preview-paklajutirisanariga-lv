Can a rug be washed in a washing machine or bath?

Can you wash a rug in a washing machine? The care label tells you: if it has a washing symbol without a cross and the rug fits in the machine, it can be washed as the label directs. Washing wool, viscose, jute and hide rugs in a machine or bath is not advisable, and for a tufted rug heavy wetting can weaken the glue in the backing. If there is no label, do not wash it and hope for the best.

Read the label first

Care symbols are set by GINETEX, and the international standard is ISO 3758. The wash-tub symbol stands for washing, and a cross over any symbol means “do not”. Bars under a symbol mean a gentler process, while letters in a circle mean that the rug may be cleaned professionally; whether it may be washed at home is shown by the tub symbol.

Many rugs have no label, or it has been cut off. Then the underside of the rug shows the fibre and the construction: whether it is woven, glued or knotted. If you do not know what the rug is made of, a washing machine experiment can cost more than cleaning.

Washing a rug in the bath

A bath seems gentler than a machine, but the rug is soaked right through in it, including the backing. Dyes can bleed, and moisture encourages that: the Canadian Conservation Institute writes that high humidity damages fibres and can make dyes bleed.

The other problem is drying. A soaked rug takes a long time to dry, and how long depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the backing, the room temperature, the air humidity and the ventilation. The US Environmental Protection Agency warns that mould in a porous material often cannot be fully cleaned and such materials sometimes have to be thrown out. More on drying: how long a rug takes to dry.

If the label allows machine washing

  1. Check the colour On a hidden spot, wet a corner and press a white cloth on it. If the colour comes onto the cloth, do not wash.
  2. Follow the label Use the temperature, programme and drying shown on the label, never warmer.
  3. Do not put it in the dryer without a symbol Use a tumble dryer only if the label has a drying symbol without a cross. Heat is a risk for a latex backing and for wool.
  4. Dry it completely Do not lay the rug back or roll it up until the backing is completely dry.

What to do if the washing went wrong

If a wool rug has shrunk and become stiff, that is felting, and it usually cannot be reversed. If the dye bled, do not wash the rug again: repeated wetting can make the dye bleed even further. If viscose pile turned brown or flattened, it may stay that way. In any case, photograph the rug from both sides and keep the label.

Frequently asked questions

Can a bath mat be washed in a washing machine?

If the label has a washing symbol without a cross, yes, as the label directs. If the mat has a latex underside, follow the label especially carefully, because heat and heavy wetting can weaken the latex glue.

Why did a wool rug become smaller and stiff after washing?

In hot water the scales of wool fibres open and, when the machine moves them, catch on each other. The material shrinks and becomes dense. This is called felting, and it usually cannot be reversed.

Can a rug be dried in a tumble dryer?

Only if the label has a drying symbol without a cross. Heat can damage a latex backing, wool and hide.

What to do if a rug smells after washing?

A smell after washing can mean that the backing has not dried yet, because thick pile and backing hold moisture for a long time. Keep the air moving and ventilate the room. If the smell stays, read why a rug smells.
