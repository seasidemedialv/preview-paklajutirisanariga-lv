Coffee and wine stains on a rug

How to remove a coffee stain from a rug: blot up the liquid at once with a clean cloth, do not rub, and use cool water, not hot. The same goes for red wine: blot, a little cool water, do not soak. A fresh stain usually comes out better than an old one, but on a light rug, or when the stain is old, traces of colour may stay.

What to do in the first minutes

  1. Blot Put a clean white cloth or paper on the stain and press lightly. Do not rub, because rubbing pushes the stain deeper. Change the cloth until it no longer picks up colour.
  2. Cool water Dampen a cloth in cool water and gently blot the stain from the edges to the middle. Do not pour water directly onto the rug.
  3. Repeat Blot and dampen again until the cloth no longer picks up colour. Do not soak the rug.
  4. Absorbent material Put a clean, dry towel on the spot, weight it down and leave it until the spot dries.

If the stain is small and fresh, these steps are often enough. Before any product, including washing-up liquid, read the label and test it on a hidden spot: the University of Georgia calls this a colourfastness test.

Sources: University of Georgia Extension, Wine (blot, cool water, do not soak, test on a hidden spot), Canadian Conservation Institute (do not rub, put on absorbent material). Checked 07.11.2026 and 10.11.2026.

Coffee and wine are not one stain

Stain First step Next What may stay
Black coffee, tea blot, check the label a little detergent foam, blot, if the label allows a brown shadow on a light rug
Coffee with milk blot, cold water do not use heat a shadow, if the milk has soaked in deep
Red wine blot, cool water do not soak, test the colourfastness pinkish traces, especially on a light rug
White wine, beer blot, cool water the same as for red wine depends on the fibre

For coffee and tea, the University of Georgia advises detergent foam, blotting and checking the label; for wine, cool water and blotting without soaking. In both cases the most important thing is as little water as possible: a rug with thick pile and backing holds moisture for a long time.

Sources: University of Georgia Extension, Coffee and tea; Wine; Blood (cold water for proteins). Checked 07.11.2026.

How to remove red wine from a rug

On a light rug red wine shows the most, so time matters most: the sooner you blot, the less wine soaks into the fibre and the backing. Work from the edge of the stain to the middle so the stain does not spread.

Salt, white wine and other home remedies are common tips, but the University of Georgia’s instructions for wine stains do not include them: they give blotting and cool water. Home tips differ between sources, and any new product on a stain can spread the colour further.

If the stain has already dried

For a dried stain blotting no longer helps, and a lot of water does not solve it: the stain can spread, and a ring can remain around it. On viscose and jute rugs, water can cause brown patches that are more visible than the stain itself.

If the stain is old, photograph it and the underside of the rug and write what it is and what you have already tried. Before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

By the rug’s fibre

  • Synthetics: the first aid is the same, with no hot water and no rubbing.
  • Wool: no hot water and no rubbing, because wool can felt and shrink. Test the colourfastness on a hidden spot before wetting it.
  • Viscose (“bamboo silk” is usually viscose): in moisture the pile can turn brown or yellow, and water with pressure can flatten it for good. Only blot, do not rub.
  • Jute and sisal: they can turn brown from water, so only blot as much as you can and do not wet them.
  • Hand-made and Persian rugs: natural dyes can bleed, so do not use products without a test.

Even in a synthetic rug, wine can leave traces if it has soaked into the backing. More on natural-fibre rugs: can a wool rug be washed at home.

Sources: University of Wisconsin Extension (wool), Cleanfax and Brentano Fabrics (viscose), makers' care instructions (jute), Canadian Conservation Institute (colours and moisture). Checked 10.11.2026 and 07.11.2026.

What not to do with a coffee or wine stain

  • Do not use hot water.
  • Do not rub the stain or scrub it with a brush.
  • Do not use chlorine bleach: it changes the colour and leaves a pale patch.
  • Do not put several products on one after another.
  • Do not soak the rug and do not dry it with a hair dryer.

Sources: University of Georgia Extension, NMSU G-402 (chlorine and strong products change the colour, an unsuitable product makes the dye bleed). Checked 07.11.2026.

What may stay and when to order cleaning

Usually comes out

  • a fresh coffee stain that was blotted at once;
  • wine on a dark synthetic rug;
  • a small stain on short pile.

May stay

  • An old red wine stain on a light rug
  • A ring around the stain after over-wetting
  • Brown patches on viscose or jute
  • A pale patch after bleach

Frequently asked questions

Should you sprinkle salt or pour white wine on a red wine stain?

In the University of Georgia’s instructions, the University of Georgia’s instructions for wine stains give blotting and cool water, not salt or white wine. Any extra product must be tested on a hidden spot, because it can make the dye bleed.

Can a coffee stain be cleaned with washing-up liquid?

For coffee the University of Georgia advises detergent foam and blotting, if the label allows it. Use only the foam, not the liquid, and test on a hidden spot first. For wool, viscose and jute it is better only to blot.

Why did a ring remain around a wine stain after cleaning?

Usually because too much water got onto the spot and the stain spread. On viscose and jute, water can also cause brown patches. Do not wet such a spot again, and send a photo.
