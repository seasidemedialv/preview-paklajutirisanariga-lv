How to clean a Persian and a silk rug at home

How do you clean a Persian or silk rug at home? The short answer: at home you care for it, you do not wash it. Vacuum regularly, blot up a fresh stain without rubbing, protect the rug from sun and moisture, and store it properly. Full wet cleaning of a silk or hand-made rug is better left to a workshop, because natural dyes can bleed and silk can be left with water marks.

Everyday care step by step

  1. Vacuum Regularly, under furniture too. In dark places a wool rug is more at risk from moths.
  2. Turn the rug From time to time turn it so that people walk on different parts and the rug wears evenly.
  3. Protect it from the sun Light dries the fibres and fades the colours. The part of a rug that lies in direct sun can fade faster.
  4. Protect it from moisture High humidity can damage fibres and make dyes bleed. Do not lay the rug in a damp room and do not leave it on a damp floor.
  5. Move it properly Do not carry the rug by one edge and do not drag furniture across it.

Source: Canadian Conservation Institute, “Basic care: carpets and rugs”; WSU PestSense on moths. Checked 10.11.2026.

If something is spilt on the rug

  1. Blot at once Press a clean, absorbent cloth on it lightly and do not rub, because rubbing pushes the stain deeper.
  2. Press down and let it dry When the excess liquid has been blotted up, put a clean, dry cloth on it and press it down with something heavy until the spot has dried.
  3. Wine on a Persian rug Blot and lightly dab with a cloth dampened in cool water, without soaking. On silk even clean water can leave a mark, so for silk only blot.
  4. Blood on a Persian rug Cold water only, never hot, because heat sets the stain.
  5. Products Test any product on a hidden spot first to see whether the colour bleeds.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute (blotting and pressing); University of Georgia Extension on wine, blood and testing a product. Checked 10.11.2026.

Can a silk rug be washed

A silk rug should not be washed at home, in a bath or in a washing machine. Silk is a natural fibre, and its main risks in moisture are water marks and dye bleeding. Without controlled moisture and complete drying, the rug can be left with patches or a bled pattern.

Before you act, make sure the rug really is silk. Rugs sold as “bamboo silk” are usually viscose. Wet viscose becomes much weaker, can turn brown, and the pile can flatten, so it has even stricter limits: it must not be rubbed or steamed.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute (moisture and colours); Ohio State University HYG-5538 and Cleanfax (viscose); Brentano Fabrics (do not rub or steam viscose pile). Checked 10.11.2026.

What not to do with a silk or Persian rug

What not to do Why
Wash it in a bath or a washing machine Dyes can bleed, wool pile can felt, silk can be left with water marks
Use shop-bought carpet shampoo Conservators do not advise it for important rugs: dirt then sticks more easily
Clean it with hot water or steam Heat strips wool of its oils, hot water with rubbing felts it
Use chlorine bleach or strong products They change the colour, a wrong product can make the dye bleed
Fold it for storage Folds leave marks and threads can break
Store it in a cellar or attic Moisture and moths

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute, UW-Extension “The Science of Felting”, University of Georgia Extension, NMSU G-402. Checked 10.11.2026.

Fringe, moths and storage

In a hand-knotted rug the fringe is the ends of the warp threads, not a sewn-on trim. So do not pull the fringe, do not cut it off and do not carry the rug by it. If it is loose or broken, that is repair work, not cleaning.

If the pile of a Persian rug is wool, keep moths in mind: clothes moth larvae feed on wool. They avoid light, so the places under furniture and rugs kept in the dark suffer most. Washington State University advises vacuuming these places regularly and cleaning the rug before storing it. If there are live moth larvae in the rug, it is a pest control job.

For storage, do not fold the rug but roll it in the direction of the pile onto a wide tube, cover it with unbleached cotton fabric or acid-free paper, and keep it off the floor. Some sources advise airtight packaging, others a breathable cover; in both cases the risk is a damp place. More: how to store a rug.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute (storage); WSU PestSense. Checked 10.11.2026.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a Persian rug be vacuumed?

Regularly, and how often depends on how much people walk on the rug. Do not forget the places under furniture: it is dark there, and moths avoid light.

Can a Persian or silk rug be steam cleaned?

No. Heat strips wool of its natural oils, and hot water together with rubbing can felt wool. On silk, moisture can leave marks, and viscose pile must not be steamed at all.

Why did the colour of a Persian rug bleed after wet cleaning?

Hand-made and antique rugs can have natural dyes that can bleed from moisture. So colourfastness must be tested on a hidden spot before the rug is wetted. Cleaning often does not restore dye that has already bled.

How can you tell whether a rug is silk or viscose?

Look at the label or the purchase documents. If the rug was sold as “bamboo silk”, it is usually viscose, and it has different risks.
