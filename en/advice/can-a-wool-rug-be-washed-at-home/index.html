Can a wool rug be washed at home?

Can a wool rug be washed at home? It usually should not be washed fully in a bath, a washing machine or with hot water: wool felts from hot water and rubbing, the rug can shrink, and it usually cannot be reversed. At home you can vacuum, blot up a fresh stain and store the rug properly. It is better to leave everything else to cleaning in a workshop.

What to do with a wool rug at home

  1. Vacuum regularly Under furniture and on the underside too, because moths more often damage wool in dark places.
  2. Turn the rug From time to time turn the rug so that the parts people walk on change and it wears evenly.
  3. Protect it from direct sun Light dries the fibre and fades the colours.
  4. Blot up a fresh stain Press a clean, absorbent cloth on it lightly and do not rub. When the excess liquid has been blotted up, put on a clean cloth and press it down with something heavy until the spot has dried.
  5. Blood only with cold water Hot water sets a blood stain.
  6. Test the product first Before using any product, try it on a hidden spot and check that the colour does not bleed.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute, “Basic care: carpets and rugs” (turning, light, blotting a stain); WSU PestSense (moths); University of Georgia Extension (blood, testing a product). Checked 10.11.2026.

Why wool must not be washed in hot water

The surface of a wool fibre is covered with tiny scales. The University of Wisconsin Extension (UW-Extension) guide “The Science of Felting” explains that in hot water the scales lift, and if the wool is rubbed or moved at the same time, the fibres catch together. This is how felt forms: the material becomes denser and shrinks.

A washing machine provides exactly these two things: warm water and movement. So a wool rug can come out of a machine smaller and denser. This process is usually irreversible.

Source: UW-Extension, “The Science of Felting” (https://oconto.extension.wisc.edu/files/2013/09/The-Science-of-Felting.pdf). Checked 10.11.2026.

Moisture, heat and dyes

In its rug care guidelines, the Canadian Conservation Institute writes that heat strips wool of its natural oils and weakens the threads, while high humidity can damage fibres and make dyes bleed. So do not dry a wool rug on a radiator, by a stove or with a hair dryer, and do not leave it damp.

The other risk is the dye bleeding. For a wool or hand-made rug the colourfastness must be tested on a hidden spot before wetting it, because dyes can bleed. Bled dye can get onto the light parts of the pattern.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute (https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/care-objects/textiles-costumes/basic-care-carpets-rugs.html); University of Georgia Extension on the colourfastness test. Checked 10.11.2026.

Moths in a wool rug

The Washington State University pest page writes that clothes moth larvae feed on wool and other materials of animal origin and avoid light. So the parts of a rug under a sofa and rugs kept in a dark place are more at risk. The advice is to vacuum and clean these places regularly, and to clean the rug before storing it. Insecticides are not an effective fix: in the long term, finding and removing the source helps.

If there are live moth larvae in the rug, it is a pest control job, not rug washing.

Source: WSU PestSense, Casemaking clothes moth (https://pestsense.cahnrs.wsu.edu/fact-sheet/casemaking-clothes-moth/). Checked 10.11.2026.

When to send a wool rug for cleaning

If the rug is grey across the whole area, if the stain is large or old, if there is urine or smell, or if the rug is hand-made or antique, home remedies usually do not help. A wool rug needs controlled wet cleaning and complete drying, which a living room floor does not give.

Frequently asked questions

Can a small wool rug be washed in a washing machine on a wool programme?

Look at the care label. If the washing symbol (a tub of water) is crossed out, it must not be washed. If there is no label, the risk is the same: warm water and movement can felt wool, and the rug can shrink.

A wool rug shrank after washing. What to do?

Felting is usually irreversible, so the size cannot be restored. Dirt and smell can still be cleaned, but cleaning does not undo the shrinkage.

Can a wool rug be cleaned with baking soda or vinegar?

Sources disagree on home remedies, but they agree on one thing: test any product on a hidden spot first and check the colour. More: rug cleaning with baking soda and vinegar.

How should I store a wool rug so that moths do not damage it?

Clean the rug before storage, roll it in the direction of the pile, do not fold it, and keep it in a dry place off the floor, not in a cellar or attic. More: how to store a rug.
