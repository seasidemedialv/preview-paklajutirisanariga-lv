Cleaning a rug with snow

How to clean a rug with snow in winter: in winter rugs are often cleaned with dry, loose snow that is sprinkled on, beaten out and brushed off. This is a custom, not a tested cleaning method, and its risks are moisture, street sand and salt. This method does not suit silk, viscose, jute and antique rugs.

How the custom is usually done

  1. Dry snow and frost The tradition calls for dry, loose snow that does not melt on the rug. Wet snow melts and soaks into the fibre and the backing.
  2. A clean spot The rug is laid on clean snow in a yard or garden, away from the road and the pavement, where the snow has sand and de-icing salt.
  3. Sprinkle and beat Snow is sprinkled on the rug, lightly beaten out and brushed off until no more dirt is left on the snow. The rug is not rubbed.
  4. Brush the snow off Before bringing the rug in, the snow is brushed off completely so that it does not melt in the pile.
  5. Dry completely Indoors the rug is left spread out until it is completely dry on the underside too, and only then is it laid back or rolled up.

These steps describe a custom, not a tested cleaning method. Snow does not replace stain treatment and does not remove what has soaked into the backing.

What can go wrong

  • Moisture. Canadian conservators write that high humidity damages rug fibres and can make dyes bleed. Melting snow is water that goes straight into the pile.
  • Wool felting. If wool gets soaked and is rubbed, the fibres can felt, and the rug can shrink. This is why the rug is beaten, not rubbed.
  • Sand and salt. Street snow often has sand and de-icing salt, and they stay in the pile.
  • Mould and smell. If a rug is rolled up or stored damp, in a dark place, it is a mould risk. Conservators advise against storing rugs in cellars and attics.
  • Heat for drying. Conservators write that heat strips wool of its natural oils and weakens the threads, so a rug is not dried on a radiator.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute, Basic care: carpets and rugs (humidity and colours, heat and wool, storage), University of Wisconsin Extension, The Science of Felting (wool felts from moisture and rubbing). Checked 10.11.2026.

Which rugs snow does not suit

Rug Why not
Viscose, “bamboo silk” wet viscose becomes much weaker and can change shape, and the pile can turn brown
Silk a natural fibre for which water marks and dye bleeding are the main risk
Jute, sisal, coir they can turn brown from water; makers advise cleaning them dry
Hand-made, Persian, antique natural dyes can bleed from moisture
Hide and fur direct contact with water damages most hides
Tufted rugs a lot of moisture can weaken the latex glue, and the backing can crumble over time

This is how synthetic short-pile rugs and wool rugs are often cleaned, but for them too the snow must be dry, and the rug must be dried completely before rolling up. If the rug is valuable and you do not know its fibre, it is better not to try.

Sources: Ohio State University HYG-5538 and Cleanfax (viscose), Canadian Conservation Institute (humidity and colours), makers' care instructions (jute), Canadian Conservation Institute, Caring for leather, skin and fur (hide), Fibreworks (tufted rugs). Checked 10.11.2026 and 07.11.2026.

Salt stains on a rug from shoes

In winter, salt and sand get into a rug even without snow cleaning, on shoes in the hallway. Blot the damp spot with a clean cloth and do not rub, because rubbing pushes the dirt deeper. Let it dry and only then vacuum up the sand.

Before storage in summer

Rugs are often rolled up and put into storage after winter. Conservators advise not folding a rug but rolling it in the direction of the pile onto a wide tube, covering it with unbleached cotton and keeping it raised off the floor, not in a cellar or attic. The rug must be clean and completely dry. More: how to wrap and store a rug.

Source: Canadian Conservation Institute (rolling, cover, place). Checked 10.11.2026.

Frequently asked questions

Can a wool rug be cleaned with snow?

Wool rugs are often cleaned this way too, but the risks are moisture and rubbing: soaked wool can felt and shrink when rubbed. The snow must be dry, the rug is beaten, not rubbed, and it is dried completely before rolling up.

Does snow remove the smell from a rug?

Snow is not smell treatment. If the smell comes from urine that has reached the backing, freshening the surface does not solve it, and the smell can come back. More: why a rug smells.

Can fitted carpet be cleaned with snow?

No, fitted carpet cannot be taken outside. It is cleaned on site: fitted carpet cleaning.

What should I do if a rug is soaked after snow cleaning?

Blot as much as you can, spread it out in a warm, ventilated room, let air reach the underside and do not put it on a radiator. More on drying: how long a rug takes to dry after cleaning.
