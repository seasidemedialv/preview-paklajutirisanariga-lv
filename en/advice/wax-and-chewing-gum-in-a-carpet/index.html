Wax and chewing gum in a rug - how to remove them

How to remove chewing gum from a rug: harden it with ice in a plastic bag and carefully scrape off the remains. Scrape candle wax off first with a blunt knife, as much as possible. In both cases do not rub, and be careful with heat: it is a risk for wool, and some synthetic fibres can melt from heat.

How to remove chewing gum from a rug

  1. Harden it Put ice cubes in a plastic bag and place it on the chewing gum. The bag keeps the ice from wetting the rug.
  2. Break it off When the chewing gum has become hard and brittle, carefully break off most of it with your fingers or a blunt knife.
  3. Scrape off the remains Scrape off the remains with a blunt knife or a spoon, working from the edge to the middle. If needed, harden it again.
  4. Check the spot If a sticky spot or colour remains, do not apply solvents. Photograph the spot.

The University of Georgia gives exactly these steps for chewing gum: harden with ice in a plastic bag and scrape off. In long pile, work more slowly and do not pull the fibres out together with the chewing gum.

Source: University of Georgia Extension, Chewing gum (ice in a plastic bag, scrape off). Checked 10.11.2026.

How to remove wax from a rug

  1. Let it harden Do not touch candle wax while it is liquid, because it spreads. Let it harden, or speed that up with ice in a bag.
  2. Scrape it off Scrape off the excess with a blunt knife as much as you can. Work from the edge to the middle.
  3. Assess what is left If a thin layer of wax is left in the fibres, you have a choice: leave it or use the heat method. Its risks are described below.

Sources: University of Georgia, Family and Consumer Sciences, Stain removal: candle wax (scraping with a blunt knife, the warm iron and paper method), Utah State University Extension (heat can push wax deeper). Checked 10.11.2026.

Iron and paper: when it is a risk

The University of Georgia’s material describes a method where paper is put on the wax and heated with a warm iron so that the paper soaks up the melted wax. Utah State University warns that heat can push wax even deeper into the fibres. So the method exists, but it is not safe for every rug.

  • Wool: Canadian conservators write that heat strips wool of its natural oils and weakens the threads.
  • Synthetics: some synthetic fibres can melt from heat.
  • Viscose: the pressure of an iron can flatten the pile for good.
  • Tufted rugs: heat can weaken the latex glue in the backing.

If you do not know the fibre or the rug is valuable, stop after scraping and send photos of the top side and the underside.

Sources: Utah State University Extension (heat pushes wax deeper), Canadian Conservation Institute (heat and wool), Brentano Fabrics (viscose pile), Fibreworks (latex glue). Checked 10.11.2026 and 07.11.2026.

What not to do with wax and chewing gum

  • Do not rub: rubbing works wax and chewing gum deeper into the fibre.
  • Do not pour solvents or oil on it: solvents can damage the backing and make the dye bleed.
  • Do not use a hair dryer to soften wax on wool and synthetics.
  • Do not put ice directly on the rug without a bag: melting ice wets the fibre.
  • Do not cut the pile with scissors if you do not know how the rug is made.

The last point matters for hand-made and tufted rugs: a hand-made rug has no layer of glue, and its threads are knotted into the backing, while in a tufted rug the threads are held by latex glue. A cut-out spot can stay visible in both, and we do not do repairs or fringe restoration.

Sources: Canadian Conservation Institute (do not rub), NMSU G-402 (solvents and backing), maker and trade pages on the construction of tufted and knotted rugs (for example Fibreworks). Checked 07.11.2026 and 10.11.2026.

By the rug’s fibre

Fibre Chewing gum Wax
Short-pile synthetics ice in a bag and scraping scraping; heat only carefully, because some fibres can melt
Long-pile (shaggy) rug ice, slow scraping, do not pull the fibres scraping; wax can get deep into the pile
Wool ice in a bag and scraping scraping; heat is a risk for wool
Viscose ice in a bag, no pressure scraping; no pressure and no heat
Jute and sisal ice in a bag, so as not to wet it scraping; water from the ice can leave a brown patch

Jute and sisal can turn brown from water, so for them the ice bag must be tied tightly shut. In long pile, wax and chewing gum can get deeper; more on these rugs: long-pile (shaggy) rug cleaning.

What may stay and when to order cleaning

Usually comes out

  • chewing gum on a short synthetic pile that was hardened at once;
  • wax that was scraped off before it was heated.

May stay

  • wax that has been pushed deep into the fibre or backing by heat;
  • fibre that has melted or flattened after an iron;
  • chewing gum that has been rubbed into long pile.

Frequently asked questions

Can chewing gum be removed from a rug with oil or a solvent?

Better not. New Mexico State University warns that solvents can damage the backing and make the dye bleed. Do not pour oil on a rug. The University of Georgia advises ice in a bag and scraping for chewing gum.

Can wax be softened on a rug with a hair dryer?

Heat can push wax deeper into the fibres and is a risk for wool, and some synthetic fibres can melt from heat. It is safer to harden the wax and scrape it off.

What should I do about coloured candle wax on a light rug?

Scrape off as much as you can and do not heat it, because heat can push the wax deeper. If a coloured spot remains, send a photo: before the job, a specialist tells you what to expect.

Can chewing gum be cut out of a long-pile rug with scissors?

A cut-out spot in a long-pile rug can stay visible, and we do not do repairs. Try ice and slow scraping, but if that does not help, send a photo.
