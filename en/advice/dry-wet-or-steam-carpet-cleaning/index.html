Dry, wet or steam rug cleaning - which suits which rug

How to clean a rug with steam depends on the fibre: for some rugs heat and moisture are fine, for others they are the main cause of damage. Viscose pile must not be steamed, makers advise cleaning jute and sisal dry, and wool can be felted by hot water together with rubbing. For synthetic fitted carpet, makers such as Shaw advise periodic professional hot water extraction.

What dry, wet and steam cleaning mean

  • Dry rug cleaning: the rug is cleaned with a very small amount of moisture. For plant-fibre rugs, makers write “dry cleaning only”.
  • Wet cleaning with extraction: a cleaning solution is introduced into the pile and drawn back out with suction together with the dirt. Carpet makers call this hot water extraction.
  • Full washing: the rug is wetted right through, including the backing. It needs controlled drying, which a living room floor does not give.
  • Steam cleaning: in everyday use this word covers different things, from hot steam to the same extraction. It matters more to know how much heat and moisture will get into the rug than what the method is called.

No method is “the best” for all rugs. What matters most is the rug’s fibre and dyes, not the name of the method or product.

Steam cleaning a rug at home: where the risk is

Steam combines two things that are dangerous for many fibres: heat and moisture. For wool, together with rubbing they mean felting, and felted wool usually cannot be restored. Water and pressure can crush viscose pile for good. Some synthetic fibres melt in heat, and for a tufted rug heat can weaken the glue in the backing.

The other risk is too much water. If moisture soaks into the backing and the underlay, the rug takes a long time to dry. How long depends on the fibre, the pile thickness, the backing, the room temperature, the air humidity and the ventilation. The US Environmental Protection Agency writes that fans and a dehumidifier speed up drying, and that porous materials in which mould has started to grow sometimes have to be thrown out. More: how long a rug takes to dry.

Dry rug cleaning: when it is the right choice

Dry cleaning is the right choice for rugs for which water is itself the damage: jute, sisal and seagrass. If the colourfastness has not been tested, do not wet the rug. Dry cleaning cannot reach dirt that has soaked deep into the rug. If urine has reached the backing, the underlay or the floor, surface cleaning may not solve the smell, and the underlay sometimes has to be replaced.

How to choose: three checks before any method

  1. Read the label Care symbols are set by GINETEX and the ISO 3758 standard. A cross over a symbol means “do not”, letters in a circle denote professional cleaning processes, and bars under a symbol mean a gentler process.
  2. Look at the underside In a tufted rug the yarn is punched through the backing fabric and fixed with latex glue, and it is often covered with a second fabric. A hand-knotted rug has no layer of glue, and the fringe is formed by the ends of the warp threads.
  3. Test the colourfastness Before using any product, try it on a hidden spot. If the colour comes onto a white cloth, wet cleaning at home is not advisable.

Care symbols: GINETEX brochure, checked 07.11.2026. Colourfastness test: University of Georgia Extension, checked 07.11.2026.

What not to do with any rug

  • Do not use shop-bought carpet shampoos on valuable rugs. The Canadian Conservation Institute writes that after such cleaning, dirt sticks to the fibres even more.
  • Do not use chlorine bleach: it changes the colour, and solvents can damage the backing.
  • Do not rub a stain. Blot it: rubbing pushes the stain deeper.
  • Do not soak the rug “just in case”: more water means longer drying and a greater risk for the backing.

Frequently asked questions

Does steam in a rug kill dust mites?

Cleaning reduces the dust and allergens in a rug, but it is not a treatment, and the mites come back while the air humidity is high. The Health Inspectorate (Veselības inspekcija) notes that mites multiply at a relative air humidity of 50–70%, so ventilation matters too.

Can fitted carpet be cleaned with steam?

Makers such as Shaw advise periodic professional hot water extraction and warn that excessive wetting without extraction can cause wrinkles and backing separation. The main thing is not to over-wet.

Which is better, dry or wet rug cleaning?

It depends on the fibre. Dry cleaning suits jute and sisal, for fitted carpet makers advise hot water extraction, and wool, viscose and silk need controlled moisture and complete drying.

Can a rug be steam cleaned if there is no label?

Better not, until it is clear what it is made of. Look at the underside and test the colourfastness on a hidden spot. If in doubt, send photos of both sides, and a specialist gives the price.
